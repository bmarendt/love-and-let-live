A Map, Not a Destination

The Opening Credo

Every episode that follows builds from this one. It isn't the show's conclusion — it's a marker that says "I am here," recorded honestly on the day it was written, so everything said afterward can be measured against it.

"No man knows the truth, and no man will, this side of death. Not this host. Not any tradition examined on this show."

Which means this show will get things wrong, out loud, on the record. Not as an accident to be apologised for later — as the expected condition of asking questions nobody can answer. When it happens, say so: hello@loveandletlive.com. Being corrected is the show working, not the show failing.

The Purpose

Why This Show Exists

This work began as a personal reckoning — the moment Blair told his wife, with total conviction, that she was going to hell, using love, in his own words, "as a weapon."

What came out of that was a question rather than an answer: what happens if the thing we call God is love? Not love as one attribute among many — love as the whole of it. This show follows that premise as far as it goes: through Jesus, through every other tradition on the map, and straight into the passages that seem to refuse it.

That premise is a point on the map, not the destination. It is where the host is standing today, held loosely, open to revision the moment something more convincing turns up.

Which raises the fair objection: if the answer can't be reached, why ask? Because the searching is the thing, not the arriving. Traditions built thousands of miles and centuries apart, with no way of reaching each other, seem on the surface to have arrived at something that resembles each other — and that is worth going to look at whether or not it ever resolves. The object of the search isn't truth. It's people: what they believe, and why, not to talk anyone out of it and not to be talked out of anything either. Anyone is welcome to come along while that continues.
